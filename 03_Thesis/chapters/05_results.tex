\paragraph{Collection.} The App Store was searched on 23 October 2026 between
19:46 and 19:53~UTC\@. Of the 49 queries, 48 returned between 64 and 190 results;
\emph{reproduktive Gesundheit} returned none. The first 30 positions held 1{,}440
hits and 848 distinct listings. Google Play was searched on 24 October 2026
between 15:07 and 15:29~UTC\@. Each query returned between 8 and 50 results, 1{,}178
in total; the first 30 positions held 1{,}138 hits and 645 distinct apps, and
every hit carried an app ID\@. The product pages of all 645 apps were read on
24 and 25 October 2026; one page that timed out was read again, so none is
missing.

\paragraph{Preinstalled apps.} The vendor shares for September 2026, read on
22 October 2026, give Samsung 39.3\,\%, Apple 27.6\,\% and Xiaomi 21.4\,\% of
German mobile page views, and Huawei and Google about 1.5\,\% each
\parencite{statcounter2026vendor}. Apple is the only vendor of iOS (27.7\,\% of
page views). On Android (72.3\,\%), Samsung leads with 54\,\% of the
platform's page views, followed by Xiaomi with 30\,\%
\parencite{statcounter2026os}. The leading vendors are therefore Apple and
Samsung. Their qualifying apps are Apple Health and Apple Fitness on the iPhone
and Samsung Health on Samsung phones. The rule therefore yields three
preinstalled apps, which leaves 47 places for the ranking. A threshold of 10\,\%
of all page views would admit Xiaomi as well, but no preinstalled tracker could
be identified on Xiaomi's international software, so the three apps are the same
under that rule and under a rule that covers 80\,\% of Android. All three have store listings, which
were removed from the ranking; Apple Health, for instance, has 2{,}134 ratings
in the German App Store.

\paragraph{Same app in both stores.} In the frames of the selection and its
checks, 946 pairs of a Google Play and an App Store listing were proposed for
review: 543 because they share only the developer, the others because they
share the app identifier, the title or description text. 308 pairs were
decided to be the same app, 636 two different apps, and 2 remain unresolved;
counting the 2 as the same app changes no selected listing. Official pages
that link both listings support 8 of the same-app pairs, and English store
names a further 18. Among the candidates reached, the decisions correct three
pairings of the earlier title rule: Nike Run Club and the BabyCenter pregnancy
app had been paired with another app of their publisher (Nike Training Club;
What to Expect), and Leap's weight-loss apps for men and for women with each
other. In 118 of the same-app pairs the two listings name different
developers, which therefore count as one provider
(Table~\ref{tab:identity-providers}). Three of these pairs join three
providers: the websites of Simple Design and ABISHKKING present the same
period, weight-loss and step-counter apps, one with their Google Play and one
with their App Store listings, and the step counter's App Store listing
belongs to the Leap Health account.

\paragraph{German-market popularity score.} Of the candidates, 189 apps were
found in both stores. The calibration used the 166 with positive rating and
review counts in both stores. Their counts correlated at Spearman 0.80, and
the median ratio was $m = 0.236412$. With the German platform shares of
September 2026, Android 72.3\,\% and iOS
27.7\,\%~\parencite{statcounter2026os}, the conversion factor was
\[
  c = 0.236412\times\frac{0.277}{0.723}\approx 0.090575.
\]
Thus, one App Store rating contributed approximately 0.09 score units and one
Play review contributed one unit. For example, komoot had 417{,}443 German App
Store ratings and 99{,}884 German Play reviews. Using the unrounded factor,
its score was $P_{\mathrm{komoot}}\approx137{,}694$. This is a combined feedback
score, not a user count; the script ranked candidates with unrounded scores.
Across all 644 Google Play candidates, the German review count and the
worldwide rating count correlated at 0.95, yet they rank the leading apps
differently: among the 100 apps with the most installs worldwide, the German
review count ranged from 5 to 99{,}884.

\paragraph{Screening and selection.} Table~\ref{tab:sample-flow} gives the
cascade. Merging the two stores' listings gave 1{,}301 candidates. In descending
order of German-market popularity score, 73 candidates were reached before
the 47 places were filled.
Of these, 12 were excluded at screening because the app records no health or
body data over time (E1), and 14 were skipped because their developer was
already in the sample. Developer names that differ between the two stores were
matched by hand for 13 providers, among them Clue, Pregnancy+, Fddb and
Headspace, each on the evidence listed in Table~\ref{tab:developer-aliases};
no store account was found under two providers, and no two providers among the
73 candidates reached named the same web domain. Across the main selection and all sensitivity checks, 276
listings were screened: 157 were eligible and 119 excluded (E1~114, E2~1,
E3~2, E4~1, E5~1).

\paragraph{The sample.} The 47 ranked apps come from 47 providers; 36 are listed
in both stores, 5 only on Google Play and 6 only on the App Store. With Apple
Health, Apple Fitness and Samsung Health, the sample holds 50 apps. By primary
category, the ranked apps comprise 21 activity apps, 13 diet and weight apps, 4
cycle trackers, 2 pregnancy apps, 3 sleep apps, 2 mood apps, 1 heart-rate app and
1 wearable companion. The selected apps have between 471 and 447{,}096 German App
Store ratings (42 apps with an App Store listing) and between 1{,}486 and
99{,}884 German Google Play reviews (41 apps with a Google Play listing). All are
free to install, and all 41 Google Play listings name a privacy policy. Of the
47 apps, 42 were found within the first ten positions of at least one query and
5 within positions 11 to 20.

\section{Reference Model}
\label{sec:results-model}
The procedure of Sections~\ref{sec:generic-model} and~\ref{sec:harm-trees} yields a model with
22 components: 3 core, 3 from the digital context, 13 implementation choices and 3 events
outside the app. They enable 12 capabilities, which lead to the 7 harm categories along 21
pathways. For 14 pathways a source states the link; 7 are argued from their sources. One
capability of the review's table stays out (K3, keystrokes from smartwatch sensors), because it
needs the connected device. Appendix~\ref{app:reference-model} lists the complete model. A tested
script checks that every node a tree uses exists and that every component is used.
