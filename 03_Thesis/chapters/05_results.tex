\section{Sample}
\label{sec:results-sample}
This section reports how the sample of Section~\ref{sec:app-sampling} was
drawn. All figures come from the collector outputs and the ranking script
(Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}); the sample is listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:app-sample}.
\todo{The screening decisions in 04\_Study/App-Sample/screening.csv were
prepared by an AI assistant from the store listings. Check every decision
before this section states who screened.}

\paragraph{Collection.} The App Store was searched on 23 October 2026 between
19:46 and 19:53~UTC. Of the 49 queries, 48 returned between 64 and 190 results;
\emph{reproduktive Gesundheit} returned none. The first 30 positions held 1{,}440
hits and 848 distinct listings. Google Play was searched on 24 October 2026
between 15:07 and 15:29~UTC. Each query returned between 8 and 50 results, 1{,}178
in total; the first 30 positions held 1{,}138 hits and 645 distinct apps. Every
hit carried an app ID and an install band, and no product page failed. The 100
apps with the highest install bands, all with at least 10 million installs,
received a product page; three further apps of that band did not.

\paragraph{Screening and selection.} Table~\ref{tab:sample-flow} gives the
cascade for each store. In rank order, 44 App Store listings and 48 Google Play
listings were reached before the 47 places were filled. Of these, 15 were
excluded at screening, 14 of them because the app records no health or body data
over time (E1) and one as a DiGA (E5). Another 30 listings were skipped because
their developer was already in the sample. Matching developers by name missed
eight providers whose names differ between the two stores (Clue, Pregnancy+,
Alarmy, Leap Fitness, Map My Run, Daylio, MeetYou and BabyCenter); these were
matched by hand, because the same app appeared under both names. Across the main
selection and all sensitivity checks, 263 listings were screened: 145 were
eligible and 118 excluded (E1~113, E2~1, E3~2, E4~1, E5~1).

\paragraph{The sample.} The 47 ranked apps come from 47 providers, 24 through
Google Play and 23 through the App Store; with Apple Health, Apple Fitness and
Samsung Health, the sample holds 50 apps. By primary category, the ranked apps
comprise 23 activity apps, 8 diet and weight apps, 7 cycle trackers, 4
pregnancy apps, 2 sleep apps, 2 mood apps and 1 wearable companion. The selected
Google Play apps have between 392{,}809 and 5{,}157{,}244 ratings worldwide; the
selected App Store apps have between 19{,}737 and 447{,}096 ratings in the German
storefront. All are free to install, and all 24 Google Play listings name a
privacy policy. Of the 47 apps, 43 were found within the first ten positions of
at least one query and 4 within positions 11 to 20; two were found by a single
keyword. Of the 24 apps selected through Google Play, 18 also appeared with
German ratings in the App Store search; 6 did not.

\paragraph{Checks.} Among the 100 Google Play apps with a product page, the
rating count and the install count correlated at Spearman 0.80, lower than the
0.96 of the trial collection, because only apps with at least 10 million
installs were included. The last app selected through Google Play has 392{,}809
ratings and lies in the lowest install band that received product pages. The
three apps of that band without a page, fetched afterwards as a check, have
314{,}218, 60{,}483 and 39{,}376 ratings, so none would have entered the
selection; apps of lower bands were not checked. Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}
shows how many of the 47 selected providers change when one rule is changed at
a time.
