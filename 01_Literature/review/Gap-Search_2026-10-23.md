# Literature gap search — 23 October 2026

A targeted search for literature the reading corpus (100 registry papers in the
[Methods Digest](../digest), 93 catalogue works, 9 core papers) does not yet
hold. The emphasis is LLM-assisted coding of privacy policies. The other digest
stages were checked for gaps against the research questions and the open
decisions in the [Decision Log](../../00_Admin/Decision-Log.md).

**Status:** reading candidates; none is in `03_Thesis/references.bib`,
`reading-status.csv` or the catalogue. The 16 priority-A papers with a PDF are
registered in the Methods Digest (nos. 101–116, with cards; §10). Metadata was
checked against the publisher, arXiv, ACL Anthology, PoPETs, USENIX or Crossref
page. Details marked *(read)* were checked in the full text; all other summaries
come from the abstract or landing page and must be checked before citing. Used by
the [app-selection evaluation](../../00_Admin/App-Selection-Evaluation_2026-10-23.md).

Downloaded open-access copies (46 PDFs, 37 from the first round and 9 from the second, §10):
[`library/`](../library/).
Priority: **A** read before freezing the method · **B** read for the related-work
chapter or a specific decision · **C** background.

## 8. What this means for the LLM design (input for D9)

These points come from the sources above; none is a decision.

1. **Pilot the hard task, not only the easy one.** Data-type detection works well with prompting (Rodriguez 2024; Xie 2025). Span-level, theory-laden annotation failed with prompting alone (Chanenson 2025: below 20 %). Harm, stance and protection spans are closer to the second case, so the pilot should report span-level recall for each axis before the model screens the corpus.
2. **Segment documents and give context.** Whole policies produced false negatives (Xie 2025, 1K-token segments). Statements depend on definitions elsewhere in the policy (PoliGraph). The paragraph unit fits, if headings and relevant definitions travel with it.
3. **Test zero-shot against definitions and examples on the pilot.** Definitions in the prompt helped most in Rodriguez 2024; examples and chain-of-thought did not help in Xie 2025. Which is better here is an empirical pilot question.
4. **Expect moderate human agreement.** Law students reached Fleiss' κ of 0.33–0.58 on German policies (Arora 2022). Acceptance thresholds should be stated per code and judged against this level, not against κ 0.8.
5. **Repeat runs even at temperature 0.** Deterministic settings still varied by up to 15 % in accuracy (Atil 2025), and some APIs do not take a seed (Mori 2025).
6. **Counts from model labels need a correction or full human checking.** Egami 2023, Gligorić 2025. The thesis already reviews all positives. The remaining risk is negatives, which the missed-mention audit addresses.
7. **A second human coder is what makes model–human claims testable.** The alternative annotator test needs at least two humans on 50–100 items (Calderon 2025). Two-model disagreement can trigger review (Georgiou 2026), but a model panel is no substitute for human validation (Brackin 2026).
8. **German.** Test the prompts on German text before the pilot, using MAPP's German policies (Arora 2022) and the German classification results in Leitner and Rehm 2025.
