                                                                      Title:
                                                       Understanding Privacy Harm Communication in Fitness Trackers


                                                         Description:
                                                            Fitness trackers collect continuous behavioral and physiological data, such as steps, heart
                                                                     rate, sleep, and location. Although these devices are often presented as wellness tools, their
                                                         data can enable sensitive inferences and downstream privacy harms. This thesis
                                                               investigates how users understand such harms and how platform privacy messages shape
                                                    what users believe is protected. The student will design and conduct an online scenario-
                                                   based survey in which fitness-tracker users evaluate possible privacy-harm scenarios before
                                                  and after reading realistic platform communication. The project is suitable for students
                                                               interested in usable privacy, HCI, empirical research methods, and privacy-enhancing
                                                             technologies.
