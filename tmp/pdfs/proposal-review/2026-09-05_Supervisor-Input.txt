Relevant Papers on Privacy Harm

https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/867
This is a paper from some of my colleagues. The paper itself is technical, but the definition of
harm and also the harm model can be quite useful in better understanding of privacy harms.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4655228
This paper further demonstrates that modern privacy risks are inference-centric rather than
collection-centric: even when companies (claim that) do not share raw data, avoid third-party
access, or keep processing on-device, they can still derive and use sensitive inferences about
individuals, which could violate user privacy and create various harms.

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3782222
The famous taxonomy with seven categories of harm.

Research Gap and Question

We lack a systematic account of what harms service providers communicate. Prior studies
examine security and privacy of health trackers, including fitness and fertility trackers, but none
map which harm types are acknowledged, minimized, or ignored, nor how PETs are framed
(e.g., secrecy vs. harm prevention). Thus, we ask:
     -​  RQ. What types of downstream privacy harms do health tracker service providers
         explicitly acknowledge or downplay when describing their privacy practices or PETs, and
     how do they frame the protection these technologies offer?

     -​  Domain:
                -​  Fitness trackers
                -​   Fertility/period tracker
     -​  Devices:
                -​  Wearables such as smart watches
