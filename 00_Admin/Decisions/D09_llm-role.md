# D9 — What does the language model do, and when is it accepted?

- **Status:** proposed (2026-11-01, author's instruction in chat: "the LLM will assist"). Model and final prompts stay open until the pilot.
- **Affects:** RQ1–RQ4 · [`04_Study/Prompts/`](../../04_Study/Prompts/README.md) · Methodology, LLM-Assisted Coding
- **Options considered:**
  1. The model codes alone. Rejected: performance depends on task and data and needs human validation (Pangakis et al. 2023, p. 1; Törnberg 2024, p. 9); configuration choices can change conclusions (Baumann et al. 2025).
  2. **The model screens and proposes; people decide (chosen).**
  3. No model. Possible, but the supervisor asked to test its usability (meeting 2026-10-13, §6).
- **Evidence (checked in the PDFs):**
  - Rodriguez et al. 2024: codebook definitions in the prompt (p. 4), two-shot examples best (p. 5), a persona did not help, temperature 0 and repeated runs to measure consistency (pp. 4–6).
  - Törnberg 2024: fixed model version (p. 3), prompt codebook (p. 7), an uncertain option (p. 8), validate only after the prompt is final and at least 20–30 examples per category (p. 10), paraphrases for prompt stability (p. 13).
  - Baumann et al. 2025: document every tested configuration (p. 18), human agreement does not license automation (p. 34).
  - Xie et al. 2025, p. 7: the prompt asks for a decision and for the supporting text. Chanenson et al. 2025, pp. 1–2: LLM annotation of privacy policies against expert labels.
  - Mayring 2025: ChatGPT reached only rough approximations of a content analysis, with gross errors (p. 1).
- **Choice:** three stages (coverage with tree nodes, stance, protection). Every code needs an exact span, which a script checks. An uncertain answer goes to manual review. Three runs per stage and one paraphrased prompt. All retained harm mentions are read by a person. Applicability and potential mismatches are decided by people only. An open-weight model is preferred if it performs close to a hosted one. Acceptance: the review triggers of the Validation section (fewer than 20 positives or recall below 0.90 means manual checking or no corpus-wide absence claim).
- **Instrument test configuration (not a model choice):** claude-sonnet-5-5 through fresh Claude Code subagents, chosen by the author on 2026-11-01 because the CLI was not logged in. Temperature and seed cannot be set in this setup.
- **Clarification 2026-11-03 (walkthrough review, D10):** the same proposed coding and blind validation apply to captured interface text and policy text. Transcripts are checked against screens before units are fixed; checking model spans cannot detect transcription errors. Report available validation units and positives by surface; policy performance alone cannot justify interface absence claims. The existing low-positive/low-recall review triggers apply to claims about interface text too. D8's second coder independently codes the captured text, which is distinct from repeating the app inspection.
- **Would change if:** the pilot shows that a stage does not reach the triggers; then that stage is coded by hand.

## Update 2026-11-04 — two models, human check of high-stakes labels, sampling validation

- **Status:** proposed (2026-11-04, author's instruction in chat after the methodology review of the same day: "the LLM has to do the coding job (or the most of it) … no human should read through all the text"; "then two LLMs"). Supersedes the "model screens and proposes; people decide every retained code" choice and the review triggers of 11-01.
- **Options considered (methodology review, 00_Admin/Reviews/Methodology-Review_2026-11-04):**
  1. Human-first coding with the model as second coder. Rejected by the author: a person would read all text.
  2. Keep the single screener, repair the validation. Weaker: misses of one model stay invisible.
  3. No model in the coding. Does not answer the supervisor's request to test model use.
  4. **Two models of different families code all units; every high-stakes label (named harm, generic risk, stance, protection framing/strength, reassurance) is checked by a person who does not see the models' labels; routine labels are kept when both models agree, otherwise uncertain; a stratified, double-coded validation sample of units measures the errors; reported figures are corrected estimates (chosen).**
- **Evidence:** Egami et al. 2023 (NeurIPS; LLM surrogate labels bias downstream estimates even at high accuracy; design-based correction with a known-probability gold sample); Angelopoulos et al. 2023 (Science 382: prediction-powered inference); Kim et al. 2025 (ICML: LLM errors are correlated, more within a provider, so two-model agreement is not reliability); Schroeder et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.15821: showing LLM labels shifts human labels and inflates measured accuracy); Wang et al. 2024 (CHI: wrong LLM labels lowered human re-annotation accuracy); instrument test (D16): 0 named harms in 178 units, 113 of 487 labels unsure.
- **Choice:** as option 4. Each model runs once per stage on the corpus; repeated runs and the paraphrased prompt are used in the pilot and on the validation sample. Validation sample strata V1 model disagreement, V2 uncertain labels, V3 harm vocabulary without a high-stakes label (replaces the separate missed-mention audit), V4 interface units, V5 plain random; selection probabilities recorded. Proposed size ≥ 500 units, ≥ 150 in V5 (to confirm after the pilot). Absence claims carry the estimated recall; with fewer than 20 reference positives recall is "not estimable" and the claim is worded as not found. Proposed: a routine code whose precision has a lower interval bound below 0.80 is reported only as a corrected estimate.
- **Would change if:** the pilot shows that a model pair cannot reach usable precision on routine codes (then that code is coded only within the validation sample and reported as an estimate), or the supervisor rejects model coding of the corpus.

## Update 2026-11-04 (evening) — estimator, sampling frame and reporting level

- **Status:** proposed (2026-11-04, after the ChatGPT submission review R1, R2, R7 and the deep research of the same day, `00_Admin/Reviews/Methodology-Deep-Research_2026-11-04/Report.md`).
- **Choice:** (1) target = the independent corpus (all units outside pilot and test apps); pilot/test apps are development material, fully coded by the author and reported separately. (2) Strata V1–V5 form a priority partition (first matching stratum), simple random sampling without replacement within strata, census of small strata; π = n_h/N_h. (3) Unit-level totals use the difference estimator T̂ = Σ_U g + Σ_S (y−g)/π with g = mean of the two models' indicators (uncertain = ½), stratified design variance; intervals describe audit uncertainty within the fixed corpus; provider bootstrap dropped. (4) App/pathway maxima are reported as detection summaries; an optional whole-app audit of high-stakes codes is the only way to measure app-level misses (decision after the pilot). (5) Joint misses estimated from all strata. (6) Single-human checked labels are distinct from double-coded reference labels. (7) α on binary unit×code indicators, unweighted; undefined if no variation; a merged/redefined code is a new version and its evaluation on the same sample is exploratory. (8) Beta-based intervals for small counts. (9) Wang et al. 2024 removed (full text unverified).
- **Evidence:** Egami et al. 2023 §2, §4; Angelopoulos et al. 2023; Horvitz & Thompson 1952; Lohr 2021 chs. 3, 5, 6; Fisch et al. 2024; Zrnic & Candès 2024; Gligorić et al. 2025; Webber 2013.
- **Still open:** sample size and allocation (pilot), the optional whole-app audit, precision threshold.

## Decision 2026-11-04 (night) — taken at the author's instruction

- **Status:** proposed — decided by the AI assistant at the author's instruction of 2026-11-04 ("finish the TODOs depending on reasoning, best practice, literature and context"); marked orange in the thesis until the author or supervisor confirms it.
- **Choices:**
  - Sample size 500 units, Neyman allocation from the pilot's stratum sizes and error spreads (Lohr 2021, ch. 3), floors of 150 units in V5 and 30 in every other stratum (or the whole stratum). If 500 units would take a coder more than 12 hours at the pilot's time per unit, the total is reduced under the same rule.
  - Variance floor (study rule): a stratum without observed errors is not treated as error-free; its error variance is at least p(1−p)m² with the Jeffreys error share p = (k + ½)/(n + 1). On a constructed corpus (40 policies × 60 units, 3 % positives, errors correlated within policies) the plain stratified formula covered the true total in 75–88 % of 1,000 draws (V5 = 150–300 units); with the floor 97–99.8 % (`scripts/validation-sample/v001/check_coverage.py`).
  - Precision bound 0.80 kept: the corrected estimate is valid at any precision; the bound only decides whether a raw count may be shown, and matches the .800 agreement convention.
  - No whole-app audit: with no miss in 5 (10) fully read apps the Jeffreys upper bound on the share of apps with a missed mention is 38 % (22 %), at about 94,000 words of reading for ten apps (9,427 words per policy in the instrument test), against the rule that no person reads the whole corpus. App-level misses are stated as not estimated.
  - Cut order kept: second walkthrough rating first, interface stratum second, never to zero (positivity); policy coding, high-stakes check and V5 never reduced.
- **Implementation:** `scripts/validation-sample/v001/` (priority partition, draw and manifest, difference estimator, joint misses from every stratum, Jeffreys intervals, Neyman allocation; 9 tests on constructed corpora).
- **Would change if:** the pilot's time per unit or error spreads differ strongly from the assumptions above; the supervisor asks for a whole-app audit.
