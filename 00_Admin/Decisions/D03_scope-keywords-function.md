# D3 — Which apps count as health trackers, and which keywords find them?

- **Status:** proposed (author's choice 2026-10-23: "use the domain of any paper that made a research on health trackers"; awaiting supervisor). Fixed before the 23 October collection. The methodology states this choice.
- **Affects:** Methodology, App Sampling; [sampling protocol](../../04_Study/App-Sample/sampling_protocol.md), current amendment; [keywords.csv](../../04_Study/App-Sample/keywords.csv); reference-model applicability and reporting across health trackers ([D15](D15_health-tracker-domain.md))
- **Options considered:**
  1. The proposal's 19 author-chosen fitness and fertility keywords (used in the 22 October trial). Rejected: no stated source, which the [app-selection evaluation](../App-Selection-Evaluation_2026-10-23.md) §3 criticised.
  2. A domain defined by store category (Health & Fitness, Medical). Rejected: categories mix trackers with content and shopping apps, and the digest recommends a functional definition (stage A, implication 3).
  3. **The domains of two published app studies on health trackers, with their keyword lists, searched in English and German, plus a functional tracking rule (proposed).**
- **Evidence:**
  - Hutton et al. (2018), *Assessing the Privacy of mHealth Apps for Self-Tracking*: no taxonomy of self-tracking apps existed, so the 20 data types most often reported by 105 London Quantified Self members became search keywords, grouped into categories (Table 2, p. 5). The mHealth categories and keywords are cycling; diet (diet, eating); exercise (exercise, workouts); heart (heartrate, heart rate); mood (mood, happiness); running (walking, running); sleep; step count (steps); weight (weight, body fat). The other categories (spending, time keeping) are not health tracking. Hutton et al. excluded instructional exercise apps "because they capture no data".
  - Hutton et al.'s survey-derived list has no menstruation or fertility category; its most popular app, a period calendar, entered only through "mood" (p. 12). The reproductive domain therefore comes from Malki et al. (2024), *Exploring Privacy Practices of Female mHealth Apps in a Post-Roe World*: terms drawn from prior work and tested in the store search, final set "female health, women's health, reproductive health, fertility, ovulation, menstrual, menstruation, period, contraception, birth control, pregnancy, pregnant" (§3.1, p. 4). Malki et al. excluded apps without a tracking or predictive feature and non-consumer apps.
  - Robillard et al. (2019) required that an app "requires the input of personal information ... over time" (§2.1), the functional tracking rule the digest recommends (stage A, approach 4).
  - Supervisor meeting of 13 October: search with German and English keywords; include mental-health trackers; explain the place of fertility and sleep tracking among health trackers ([organized notes](../Meeting-Notes/2026-10-13_Supervisor-Meeting_Organized.md) §1). Hutton et al.'s mood and sleep categories and Malki et al.'s reproductive terms cover these.
- **Choice (proposed):**
  - **Domain.** Health trackers overall, as confirmed by the author on 28 October 2026 ([D15](D15_health-tracker-domain.md)). The mHealth categories of Hutton et al. (2018) and the reproductive-health scope of Malki et al. (2024), including pregnancy tracking, supply the operational scope definition and keywords. They do not divide the study into separate populations or analysis strata.
  - **Keywords.** The 15 mHealth keywords of Hutton et al. and the 12 terms of Malki et al., each searched in the original English and in one German translation of the same data type; identical spellings (`workouts`, `menstruation`) and repeated translations are searched once: 49 queries ([keywords.csv](../../04_Study/App-Sample/keywords.csv)). Translations follow the tracked data type rather than the word (`eating` → `Ernährung`).
  - **Function rule (eligibility E1).** The app lets the user record personal health or body data over time, by input or sensor, in at least one of these categories (Robillard et al. 2019; Malki et al. 2024). Instructional or content-only apps, one-off calculators, shops, games without such recording and general-purpose apps are excluded (Hutton et al. 2018).
  - **Boundary cases.** Companion apps of wearables are eligible when the app itself records or displays the tracking data; the device is not analysed (D11, meeting: apps only). Consumer apps for the general public only (Robillard et al.; Malki et al.). Chronic-disease, snoring and other specialised trackers are inside the domain only where they record data in one of the categories; they are not searched for separately, because neither source lists them.
  - Each app's primary category is recorded as a descriptor. There are no quotas (D1).
- **Reason:** A domain and keyword list taken from published health-tracker studies replaces an author-chosen list with a stated, survey-derived source (Hutton et al.) and a store-tested one (Malki et al.), and covers the meeting's request for mental-health (mood) and sleep trackers. Searching each term in English and German follows the meeting. The function rule makes "tracker" a property of the app, not of its store category.
- **Operational checks (26 October 2026, at the author's request; they state how the screening of 23–25 October applied E1–E5, no rule changes):** each criterion is checked on the store listing in the German storefront (title, developer, genre, price, description).
  - E1: the description states that the app records, logs or stores the user's own measurements or activities in a domain category, entered by the user or recorded by a sensor or paired device ([sampling protocol](../../04_Study/App-Sample/sampling_protocol.md) v2).
  - E2: the listing offers the app to the general public; it is excluded when the listing limits use to the members, patients, employees or professional users of an organisation (screening log: *Meine AOK*, a statutory insurer's service app for its members).
  - E3: price 0 — App Store: the Search API's `price` for the German storefront (EUR); Google Play: the product page's `price` 0 and `free` true (D4). In the 23–24 October collections the two Play fields agree for all 645 apps.
  - E4: the listing text in the German storefront is in German or English (screening log: an Arabic-only pregnancy calculator).
  - E5: the listing describes prescription by a doctor or reimbursement by statutory health insurance; such an app is checked against the BfArM DiGA directory (sampling protocol v2).
  - An app listed in both stores is decided on one screened listing; where both listings were screened, an exclusion in either excludes the app (`app-ranking/v004`, `decision`). Corrected 26 October: an earlier wording said every criterion had to hold in both stores, but 38 of the 125 both-store candidates were decided on one listing. In the main selection, 27 had both listings screened and the decisions never differed, so the rule excluded no app. In the App Store depth-200 sensitivity variant the title matcher paired Fitbit (Play) with the Google search app and Google Fit with Chrome (same provider, same first word), and the excluded App Store listings excluded both; the variant's provider count did not change (Google stays in through another listing), but the matcher's first-word rule is a known weakness.
- **Consequences (confirmed by author, 28 October 2026):** one study domain, health trackers. The pilot, validation, walkthroughs and reporting operate across that sample without category allocations. Pathway applicability depends on each app's features and context. The former open concern about category-specific allocations is superseded by [D15](D15_health-tracker-domain.md); keyword sources and descriptive categories remain documented.
- **Would change if:** the agreed health-tracker scope or search coverage changes, or the screening log shows that the function rule cannot be applied consistently from store pages. Any change needs a dated decision and an assessment of the existing sample.

## Update 2026-11-06 — E1 expanded, keyword work recorded

- **E1 expanded (applied; no change of rule).** The supervisor asked for E1 to be broken into sub-items that explain Hutton et al. and are as clear as E3. Methodology Table 4.4 now has E1a records data, E1b the user's own health or body data, E1c over time, E1d in a category of the domain, all four required. The sub-items only restate the function rule above and the operational check of 26 October; they carry the exclusions each source gives (Hutton et al., p. 5 and Table 2: instructional apps captured no data, spending and time keeping are not health; Robillard et al. §2.1: input at one time point only; Malki et al. §3.1: no tracking or predictive feature). The 23–25 October screening is unchanged; it was not re-run.
- **Categories are keywords only (applied).** The categories of Hutton et al. and Malki et al. group the search keywords; the thesis names no subcategories of health trackers, and their overlap (exercise, heart rate) does not matter, because eligibility asks for at least one category, whichever keyword found the app. This restates [D15](D15_health-tracker-domain.md).
- **Open (recorded in the meeting notes §5, not applied):** reverse engineering of the keyword set (interpretation: read terms and related apps from the listings of apps that are certainly trackers); a manageable result count per keyword; a test of the keywords and of the store API; three apps that must be included; a check, after the 50 apps are picked, of which keyword categories of Table B.1 the sample misses (a result, not a method rule). The 49 queries and the first-30 depth of 23 October stay as they are until the author decides.
