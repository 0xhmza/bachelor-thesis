# D5 — Do apps without a reachable privacy policy stay in the sample?

- **Status:** confirmed (2026-10-13, supervisor meeting; adopted by the author 2026-10-23). The methodology states this choice.
- **Affects:** Methodology, App Sampling (eligibility) and Analysis and Reporting (denominators); [sampling protocol](../../04_Study/4.4_App-Sampling/sampling_protocol.md) v2, which replaces v1 exclusion criterion 1
- **Options considered:**
  1. Proposal: a reachable policy is an inclusion criterion; absence is logged, then the app is excluded.
  2. **Include the app and report the absence as a finding (chosen).**
- **Evidence:**
  - Supervisor meeting of 13 October: "Include apps without a privacy policy (PP), and report that absence in the results" ([organized notes](../Meeting-Notes/2026-10-13_Supervisor-Meeting_Organized.md) §1).
  - Precedents keep absence as an outcome: Sunyaev et al. (2015) record absence after a three-step discovery protocol; O'Loughlin et al. (2019) keep all eligible apps in the denominator (digest, stage A, approach 5).
- **Choice:** a privacy policy is not an eligibility criterion. Its presence is recorded at sampling where the store lists a policy URL and checked at collection with a fixed discovery procedure; an app without one stays in the sample, and the absence is reported.
- **Reason:** The supervisor set the rule, and excluding apps without a policy would remove exactly the cases in which providers communicate least.
- **Would change if:** the supervisor revises the rule.
