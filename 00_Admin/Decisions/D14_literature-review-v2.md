- **Options considered:**
  1. D13: the staged procedure of Baumann et al. on OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, with a filter of at least three citations per year.
  2. **Venue-anchored search: the supervisor's venues enumerated in full, established bibliographic databases searched with one Boolean query per review sub-question, one round of citation searching, and every thesis citation and Methods Digest paper screened with the same rules (chosen).**

- **Choice:**
  - *Review question* (one, with four sub-questions LQ1–LQ4 tied to RQ1–RQ4 and to the methodology): what is known about the privacy harms and capabilities associated with health tracking apps, how their privacy communication has been analysed and what it was found to say, how language models have been used and validated for coding privacy text, and how privacy texts frame risk and protection?
  - *Strands:* S1 = D AND P AND H (LQ1); S2 = D AND P AND C (LQ2); S3 = T AND L AND A (LQ3, from 2022); S4 = T AND F (LQ4); S5 = German-language D AND P (PubMed only). Term groups and their translations in [`config.json`](../../scripts/literature-search/v002/config.json) and `lit.py`.
  - *Sources:* databases PubMed, IEEE Xplore, arXiv, ACL Anthology (title and abstract); venues PoPETs, USENIX Security, SOUPS, CCS, IEEE S&P, NDSS, CHI, CSCW, enumerated in full for 2014–2026 and filtered locally (venue rules in `config.json`); citation searching (one round, backward and forward, OpenCitations) from the four health-tracker papers the supervisor sent; every work cited in the thesis; every Methods Digest paper.
  - *Period:* 1 January 2014 to 28 October 2026; S3 from 1 January 2022.
  - *Deduplication:* shared DOI, arXiv ID, or normalised title with years at most one apart.
  - *Screening:* eligibility criteria and the ordered exclusion rules R1–R8 of [`01_Literature/Literature-Review/README.md`](../../01_Literature/Literature-Review/README.md); one primary reason per record (the first rule that applies); titles first, abstracts where the title does not decide, full text for records that pass.
  - *Contextual collection:* thesis citations that fail a rule are kept, marked contextual, with the rule that excluded them. They are not review evidence.
  - *Extraction and synthesis:* per included study, sub-question, domain, surface, method, harms and capabilities named; an evidence map links studies to RQ1–RQ4. Capabilities from LQ1 studies feed the harm trees.
  - *Quality appraisal:* none as an eligibility rule; peer-review status is recorded and reported.
- **Reason:** The venue enumeration grounds the search in the supervisor's venues and makes their coverage complete rather than dependent on a database's indexing; the databases add the health-informatics (PubMed), engineering (IEEE Xplore), NLP (ACL) and preprint (arXiv) literature the venues do not hold. Baumann et al.'s structure (term groups, exact strings per database, hand-added works reported separately, explicit criteria, fixed extraction) is kept because it makes the search repeatable. Their citation filter is not kept: it removed 611 of 648 records from 2026 in the D13 run, while the closest precedents of this thesis are from 2024–2026, and citation counts differ by database. One query per sub-question replaces their single query because the review serves four parts of the study. Their Stage 3 (dataset selection) becomes the capability table for the harm trees.
- **Limits:** One person screens; the screening was prepared by an AI assistant and must be checked by the author. ACM DL, Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar were not searched; CHI, CSCW (until 2016), CCS, IEEE S&P and NDSS were filtered on titles only, because Crossref holds no abstracts for them. Queries are in English (the German PubMed strand returned no records).
- **Would change if:** the supervisor names other venues or confirms "mdmp"; institutional access to Scopus or Web of Science, or a working ACM DL session, becomes available (run the prepared strings and add the records through the same rules); the author's check changes more than a few screening decisions.
