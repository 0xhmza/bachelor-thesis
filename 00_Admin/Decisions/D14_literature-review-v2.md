- **Choice:**
  - *Review question* (one, with four sub-questions LQ1–LQ4 tied to RQ1–RQ4 and to the methodology): what is known about the privacy harms and capabilities associated with health tracking apps, how their privacy communication has been analysed and what it was found to say, how language models have been used and validated for coding privacy text, and how privacy texts frame risk and protection?
  - *Term groups:* D (health tracking apps and wearables), P (privacy), H (harms, threats and capabilities), C (communication surfaces), T (privacy text), L (large language models), A (annotation and analysis tasks), F (framing); terms joined with OR. Terms in [`v003/config.json`](../../scripts/literature-search/v003/config.json).
  - *Strands:* S1 = D AND P AND H (LQ1); S2 = D AND P AND C (LQ2); S3 = T AND L AND A (LQ3); S4 = T AND F (LQ4). No German-language strand.
  - *Sources:* venues PoPETs, USENIX Security, SOUPS, CCS, IEEE S&P, NDSS, CHI and CSCW, listed in full for 2014–2026 (Crossref, usenix.org) and filtered locally: D OR S3 OR S4 at the security and privacy venues; S1 OR S2 OR S3 OR S4 at CHI and CSCW where abstracts are held; (D AND P) OR (D AND H) OR (D AND C) OR (T AND L) OR (T AND F) on titles only (CHI, CSCW to 2016). Databases PubMed and IEEE Xplore (one query per strand, title and abstract), arXiv (anchor queries, strands applied locally to the pool) and the ACL Anthology (its bibliography file, strands applied locally). Citation searching: one round, backward and forward (OpenCitations Index, metadata from Crossref), from four seed papers (Malki et al. 2024, Hudig and Singh 2025, Cao et al. 2024, Salehzadeh Niksirat et al. 2024).
  - *Period:* 1 January 2014 to 28 October 2026; S3 from 1 January 2022.
  - *Deduplication:* automatic (`merge.py`): records share a DOI or an arXiv ID, or have identical normalised titles with publication years at most one apart (three when one is an arXiv preprint). Each record keeps every source that found it.
  - *Screening:* C1 research article reporting original empirical or analytical work, or a systematic or scoping review; C2 written in English; C3 published in the period; C4 answers at least one of LQ1–LQ4. Ordered exclusion rules R1–R8 (Table "Eligibility criteria and exclusion rules" of Section 4.1), one primary reason per record (the first rule that applies); titles first, abstracts where the title does not decide, full text for records that pass. R7: the full text requires payment and is available neither through the university's institutional access or network nor as a publicly accessible version such as an arXiv preprint. Abstracts that no source holds are taken by DOI from OpenAlex, for screening only.
  - *Extraction and synthesis:* for every record that passes title-and-abstract screening, the sub-question (from screening) and the app domain, material studied and venue type (fixed keyword rules, `extract.py`); for the included studies, by hand from the full text, the parts of the RQ, the harms named and, for LQ1, the capabilities, grouped into categories with the data needed and the actor (input to the harm trees). Synthesis: counts by year and venue, an evidence map of domains against materials and RQ parts, and a prose summary per sub-question.
  - *Quality appraisal:* none as an eligibility rule; peer-review status is recorded and reported.
- **Reason:** The venue enumeration makes coverage of the main security, privacy and HCI venues complete rather than dependent on a database's indexing; the databases add the health-informatics (PubMed), engineering (IEEE Xplore), NLP (ACL) and preprint (arXiv) literature the venues do not hold; citation searching adds studies the term groups miss. Baumann et al.'s structure (term groups, exact strings per database, explicit criteria, fixed extraction) is kept because it makes the search repeatable. Their citation filter is not kept: it removed 611 of 648 records from 2026 in the D13 run, while the closest precedents of this thesis are from 2024–2026, and citation counts differ by database. One query per sub-question replaces their single query because the review serves four parts of the study. Their Stage 3 (dataset selection) becomes the capability table for the harm trees. The changes of 30 October follow the author's instructions listed under *Evidence*; the German strand had returned no record.
- **Limits:** One person screens; the screening was prepared by an AI assistant and must be checked by the author. ACM DL, Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar were not searched; CHI, CSCW (until 2016), CCS, IEEE S&P and NDSS were filtered on titles only, because Crossref holds no abstracts for them. Queries are in English only. No direct published precedent was found for two rules, which are kept because they were applied: the domain group alone selects LQ1 and LQ2 candidates at the security and privacy venues, and arXiv is searched through an anchor pool with the strands applied locally. In the PubMed S3 string, `gpt*` has fewer than the four characters PubMed requires before a wildcard; in the arXiv anchors, `wearable*` returned no record. Both are to be checked.
