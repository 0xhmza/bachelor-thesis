# D15 — One study domain: health trackers

- **Status:** confirmed by the author on 28 October 2026 in this chat. This is an author clarification, not a recorded supervisor approval.
- **Author's instruction:** "remove anything related to the two domains: fitness and fertility. the domain of the study is health trackers overall. the split was just in domain definition and keyword collection from previous papers."
- **Evidence:** the author's instruction above and the 13 October supervisor guidance to include mental-health trackers and use no category quotas. D3 records the published sources of the scope definition and search terms.
- **Choice:** the study has one domain, health trackers. The categories and terminology borrowed from Hutton et al. and Malki et al. explain the scope definition and keyword collection. They do not define separate study populations, strata, reference frameworks or mandatory reporting groups.
- **Consequences:** retain the existing totals, applied to the whole sample: five pilot apps, ten independent validation apps and twelve walkthrough apps (the walkthrough total was changed to all 50 apps on 2 November 2026, see D10). Remove category allocations from the pilot and validation. Retain recorded random seeds, pilot/validation separation by provider and document, and language stratification where both policy languages occur. Walkthroughs retain the existing overall platform balance of six Android and six iOS apps, filled alternately in rank order without reusing an app; there are no category slots.
- **Reference model and reporting:** build the reference harm trees for health trackers from the relevant literature. Assess each pathway against each app's features, data, access conditions and context. Categories can describe the sample; they do not select a separate reference tree or impose separate analyses. This clarification does not settle D12's open question about the supervisor's phrase "best app".
- **Unchanged evidence:** the 49 search queries, raw collections, eligibility decisions, app ranking, selected apps and category counts are not changed by this clarification. A single study domain does not imply exhaustive keyword coverage of all health tracking.
- **Supersedes:** the category-specific allocations and reference-tree/reporting split in the current methodology, codebook v3 and walkthrough protocol v2; D3's open "Consequences" paragraph; the corresponding open concern in the 23 October evaluation and action plan; findings F18 and F19 of the sampling peer review as prospective design objections.
- **Implementation:** methodology and Appendix A; codebook v4; walkthrough protocol v3; current sampling-protocol amendment; study README; D1/D3 and the decision register; both review reports. Historical proposal, meeting notes, correspondence and earlier codebook versions remain records of their original dates.
- **Current walkthrough amendment (2 November, made explicit in the 3 November review):** D10 supersedes both the twelve-app total and the six/six platform balance in the historical Consequences paragraph above. Attempt all 50 apps; the platform rule stays open for the pilot. The current instruments are codebook v5 and walkthrough protocol v7. The Implementation paragraph records the versions used for the 28 October scope change, not today's versions.

## Update 2026-11-06 — no subcategories, only keywords

The supervisor asked whether overlapping categories (exercise and heart rate) are a problem. The paper has no subcategories of health apps: the categories of Hutton et al. and Malki et al. group the search keywords and describe the sample, nothing more. The methodology (Domain paragraph, E1d) and the caption of the keyword table now say so. An app is eligible once it meets E1, whichever keyword found it ([D03](D03_scope-keywords-function.md)). After the 50 apps are picked, the results will say whether any keyword category is not represented.
