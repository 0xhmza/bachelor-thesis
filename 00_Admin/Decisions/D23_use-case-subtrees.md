- **Question:** how is the reference harm tree built so that it is the most general tree the evidence supports, can be argued step by step, and costs one analyst a bounded effort per step?
- **Options considered:**
  1. One step: list every function of a tracker and build the seven trees at once (D12 as first applied). Rejected as the *argument*: the reader sees the finished tree but not why each part is there or when the list stops.
  2. A tree per app, for 3 or for 50 apps. Rejected: trees would repeat the same capabilities and could not be reused; the apps differ in what they do, not in what the harms are (Knabenhans et al. reuse sub-trees across use cases instead).
  3. **One subtree per use case, built in sequence, each reusing what the earlier ones defined; the general tree is their union (chosen).**
- **Evidence:**
  - Knabenhans et al. 2026, §4 (p. 6): harms cannot be judged from the abstract functionality; "it is necessary to consider concrete use cases". §4.1 (p. 6–7): trees from system components, capabilities and harms; origin colours; no claim of completeness, further pathways "only provide further evidence". §4.2 (p. 7): the first use case yields two *common sub-trees* (service stops, tracking and profiling) that several harm trees reuse. §4.3 (p. 8): the second use case reuses trees verbatim ("apply verbatim"), simplifies others and adds one. §4.4 (p. 9): further requirements add components to existing trees. §4.5 (p. 10): each tree keeps a pathway without red components.
  - Mauw and Oostdijk 2005: the set semantics of AND/OR trees; used for the mechanical derivation below.
  - The ten papers of the literature review document the functions that become use cases (pages in the cards of `use_cases_v1.yaml`).
- **Choice (procedure):**
  1. *Use case.* A function that a paper of the review documents for health trackers (D12 rule), with its documenting sources. Order: from nothing leaving the phone to the most external actors and to the user's own constraints. The order does not change the final tree (it is a union of monotone formulas; tested), only the accounting of when something appears.
  2. *Card.* Six fixed questions: what is **recorded**, what is **derived**, who can reach the records on the **phone**, what **leaves** the phone to whom, what the app **asks** of the user, which event **outside** the app a pathway assumes. Each answer names components of the model (existing ones are reused) and carries page evidence and its basis (documented or argued). Origin colour by the test of the methodology (core, digital context, implementation choice, outside event).
  3. *Derivation, not judgement.* Capabilities are typed formulas over components (AND/OR, no OR+); a capability becomes possible when its formula holds for the components known so far; a pathway follows when its capability is possible. The script computes this, and records for each step the new components, the new capabilities, the new routes to existing capabilities (new minimal sufficient sets) and the new pathways. The analyst judges only components, evidence and the first definition of a new capability.
  4. *Stopping rule.* Stop when two consecutive further use cases ("check cases") introduce no component, capability, route or pathway. The check cases are processed *after* the model stood, so they show that the six questions leave no answer unmapped; they are not an independent test.
  5. *Generality.* The general tree is the union of the subtrees, with one definition per capability. By monotonicity every subtree is contained in it, and the tree of any real app is its restriction to the components that app has (the applicability step). It is general over the use cases the sources document, not complete (Knabenhans et al., §4.1).
- **Reason:** the same argument as Knabenhans et al. (harms depend on who receives what, so use cases), made incremental so that every component, capability and pathway can be traced to a use case and a page, and so that the effort per use case is a card plus a check by script instead of a one-step enumeration. The script verifies closure (every component introduced exactly once with evidence, every capability and pathway reached, check cases add nothing, citation keys exist, pages given); 14 unit tests.
- **Known limits (stated, not hidden):**
  - The cards were written by the AI assistant, after reference model v2 existed, from the ten papers (pages found by text search of the extracted PDFs, not read in full). The run therefore shows that the model is *derivable* from use cases with page evidence, not that it was *discovered* this way. Check the cards before the thesis states who wrote them.
  - Same-source dependence: all use cases and the check cases rest on the same ten papers. An independent check would use sources outside the ten (for example Grundy et al. 2019, cited in the K10 note of the model) or a second person building cards without seeing the model.
  - Earlier text said "22 components, 13 implementation choices"; model v2 has 23 and 14 (IM14 was added on 4 November). Results 5.3 now states 23 and 14.
  - Pages are those of the PDF page. The cards were checked for the figures they quote (for example 13 of 19 policies, 29 of 36 apps), not for every sentence.
