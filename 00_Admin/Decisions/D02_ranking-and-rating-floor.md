# D2 — How are eligible apps ranked, and what is the rating floor?

- **Status:** proposed (author's choice 2026-10-22, after the storefront table; awaiting supervisor). Revised the same day from a blended score to exposure only, and from a percentile merge to equal alternation between stores. The methodology states this choice. Sample size and preinstalled apps are in [D1](D01_sample-size.md) and [D6](D06_preinstalled-apps.md). **23 October:** the search frame (depth, session) and the selection unit (one app per developer) were added as proposed choices before the collection of that day, see "Search frame and selection unit" below; the keywords are in [D3](D03_scope-keywords-function.md). API order versus store-UI order remains unverified and is stated as a limitation.
- **Affects:** RQ-sampling · [`scripts/app-ranking/v001/`](../../scripts/app-ranking/README.md) · Methodology, stage A (app search and selection)
- **Options considered:**
  1. **Draft rule:** floor of 1,000 ratings, then rank by average rating, rating count as tie-break.
  2. **Exposure only (proposed):** within each store, rank eligible apps by rating count; take apps alternately from the two ranked lists (earlier draft: merge by within-store percentile).
  3. **Blend:** combine the exposure rank with a shrunk average rating. Kept only as a sensitivity check.
  4. **Downloads in the score:** rejected for now, see Evidence.
- **Evidence:**
  - The thesis claim is about exposure to privacy communication, so selection should answer "which apps do people meet", not "which apps do people like". Hutton et al. (2018) found that popularity measures (downloads, stars, number of ratings) did not predict privacy (card `hutton2018`, p. 6). Popularity is therefore a filter for exposure and not a quality signal, and an average rating has no stated link to what is being studied.
  - The sampling rules in the digest cards rank by counts, rank positions or download bands, and none ranks by average rating: the 300 most frequently rated apps per store (Sunyaev et al. 2015, card p. 3), reviews in the preceding 12 months (Malki et al. 2024, p. 4), search rank as popularity proxy with a rank-stability check (Huckvale et al. 2019, p. 2), the seven most popular apps per category (Hutton et al. 2018), up to the top 250 per category (Arkalakis et al. 2024), weekly top-100 lists (Tangari et al. 2021; Grundy et al. 2019). Alfawzan et al. (2022) used rating values only to justify a floor, because few ratings mean high variance (p. 5). These are summaries from the digest cards, not rechecked against the papers.
  - Supervisor meeting of 13 October: "ratings or downloads: rating makes more sense, exclude if downloaded less than x times" ([raw notes](../Meeting-Notes/2026-10-13_Supervisor-Meeting_Raw.md)). The wording does not say whether rating count or average is meant, so this needs confirmation.
  - No verified German download figure exists for either store. Apple's search API returns ratings but no downloads. The Play integer is global, not German. Sensor Tower estimates covered 15 of 169 apps before rate-limiting (sampling report (removed on 25 October 2026; in Git history); [scraper README](../../scripts/appstore-scraper/README.md)).
  - Rating count as a proxy for downloads: Sunyaev et al. (2015) used it on iOS, which reports none, and on Android the two correlated at Spearman r = 0.89 (card `sunyaev2015`, p. 3).
  - **Storefront check, 22 October 2026** (public Apple lookups and Google Play product-page requests, 6 Apple apps and 5 Play apps, storefronts DE, US and GB, one session):
    - Apple's rating count and average are storefront-specific. Examples (DE / US / GB): Yazio 446,768 / 51,379 / 12,351; Google Gemini 270,417 / 2,363,183 / 241,116; MyFitnessPal 80,176 / 2,381,357 / 474,412.
    - Google Play's rating count and install figure were identical across storefronts (for example Home Workout: 4,045,258 ratings in DE and US, 4,045,260 in GB). The average rating and the undocumented embedded review count varied (MyFitnessPal reviews: 32,560 in DE, 454,136 in US). The `gl` parameter therefore does not make the Play rating count German, and the only other route, the reviews RPC endpoint, falls under a path that `robots.txt` disallows (mechanism note (removed on 25 October 2026; in Git history)).
    - **Correction (same day, second check of public first-party pages, one app per store in DE and US):** the Play one-to-five-star histogram is *not* worldwide. Its bins differ by country (MyFitnessPal one-star: 428,497 in DE, 205,443 in US), their weighted mean equals the country's average rating (3.94 and 4.41), and they sum to the worldwide count. It is the country's distribution scaled to the worldwide total. The first check had compared only the sums.
    - Fields the first check missed: the App Store product page (`apps.apple.com/<country>/app/...`) embeds a one-to-five-star breakdown for the storefront that the API lacks (Yazio five-star: 349,509 in DE, 42,759 in US), and both stores show a chart-position badge for the storefront (Yazio no. 3 in Health & Fitness on the German App Store; MyFitnessPal no. 2 top grossing in Health & Fitness on the US Play store, none in DE). Apple's category chart pages (for example `apps.apple.com/de/iphone/charts/6013?chart=top-free`) list the storefront's top apps. Neither store publishes a written-review count that the store itself documents, and neither publishes German download counts; Apple and Google report downloads to the developer only ([Apple acquisition help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition), [Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/139628)). Apple documents one summary rating per country or region ([Apple ratings help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/monitor-ratings-and-reviews/ratings-and-reviews-overview)); Google announced country-specific phone ratings from November 2021 ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2021/08/making-ratings-and-reviews-better-for.html)).
    - Access: `itunes.apple.com/robots.txt` disallows `/search*` (the path of the Search API the collectors use), `/*/lookup?` and `/*/rss/*`. `apps.apple.com` product and chart pages are allowed. On Google Play, `/store/search`, `/store/getreviews` and `/_` are disallowed and product pages are allowed. Checked 22 October 2026. Apple documents the Search API for programmatic use; whether the robots rule applies to it is an open point for the search frame.
    - A chart position is a store-made, market-specific popularity signal that could serve as an alternative or a check for exposure. It is not part of the proposed choice below.
    - The tests are small and were run once, so they rule out a scraper setting that fixes the Play count but do not describe every app. The scratch scripts are not part of the repository.
  - Alfawzan et al. (2022) justified a floor with data and checked the next excluded apps (p. 5). The same check is needed on the real candidate pool.
  - **Evaluation, 23 October 2026** ([App-Selection-Evaluation](../App-Selection-Evaluation_2026-10-23.md), Play pilot re-analysed with `scripts/sampling-checks/v001/`): Leap Fitness Group holds 10 of the Play top 47 under one policy URL; the 24 Play places hold 3 period trackers and 2 pregnancy apps; 13 of the top 47 were found only at search positions 11–20; the 1,000 floor does not decide any selected app (47th Play app: 309,727 ratings). Proposed changes are listed there; none is adopted here.
  - Pilot run on the existing v005 trial output (`fitness` and `fitness sleep-tracker`, Apple, German storefront, 185 listings, 98 above the floor, unfiltered by function, language or payment): blending in the shrunk rating changed the top 20 by 3 apps at exposure weight 0.75, by 8 at 0.5 and by 11 at 0.25. Ranking by rating count per year since release changed 1 app. This demonstrates the mechanics only and is not a sample.
- **Choice (proposed):**
  - Eligibility first: the functional rules from D3, D4, D5 and D7, applied as a manual exclusion list, plus a minimum number of ratings (default 1,000, to be confirmed). The floor is argued as a reliability threshold for the exposure proxy, in line with Alfawzan et al. (2022).
  - Within each store, rank eligible apps by rating count (log10 for the recorded exposure value; ranks are unchanged by the log). One ranking per store covers both domains; there are no domain quotas (D1). Ties break on listing ID.
  - Store balance: take apps alternately from the two ranked lists, starting with Google Play (Android runs on most German phones: Samsung 39.3 %, Xiaomi 21.4 % against Apple 27.6 % of mobile page views in September 2026, [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/vendor-market-share/mobile/germany)). Skip an app already selected through the other store; it counts once and both listings are captured. Stop at 47 ranked apps (D1). Raw counts are never compared across stores.
  - Precedent for equal store weight: Sunyaev et al. (2015) took the 300 most frequently rated apps from each store; O'Loughlin et al. (2019) capped the App Store at the 250 results Google Play shows so that results "would not be biased towards findings from the iTunes App Store" (both checked against the PDFs on 2026-10-22). Proportional allocation by operating-system share was rejected because it would rest on page-view shares and would still compare counts on different bases.
  - Proxy check: on the Google Play candidates, report the Spearman rank correlation between the worldwide rating count and the worldwide embedded install count (and the install band), replicating Sunyaev et al.'s check. The App Store publishes no downloads, so the check rests on Google Play. Sunyaev et al. report only the coefficient (0.89, p < 0.001); the paper does not describe the download measure or the sample behind it. **Pilot, 22 October 2026:** the v002 Play collector on all 19 keywords (German storefront, `language` de, first 20 results each, no detail errors) found 190 distinct apps, 159 with both figures. Spearman between rating count and embedded install count: 0.959 (n = 159); against the install band: 0.949; among the 135 apps with at least 1,000 ratings: 0.936. Output: `scripts/googleplay-scraper/v002/output/pilot-de-19kw.json` (git-ignored; back it up with the raw data). This uses the search page that `robots.txt` disallows, at the author's direction.
  - Apple counts come from the German storefront. Google Play counts are worldwide and are stated as such in the thesis; the two stores therefore measure exposure on different bases, which the separate rankings and the alternation keep apart. Summing the Play histogram does not give a German count: its bins are the country's distribution scaled to the worldwide total.
  - The average rating, the Play review count (storefront-specific, sparse, not validated as an exposure proxy) and the Play install band are recorded as fields and used only in sensitivity checks. Downloads and API position are not used in the ranking.
  - Sensitivity, reported with the result: blends with the shrunk average rating (exposure weight 0.75, 0.5, 0.25) and rating count per year since release. For each, record which apps enter or leave the top N and the rank correlation.
- **Search frame and selection unit (proposed 2026-10-23, before collection):**
  - **Session.** Both stores searched in one session, logged out, German storefronts: Apple's Search API with `country=de` (iPhone-compatible results) and the Google Play search page with `gl=de`, `hl=de`, which approximates a German user's store. The 49 queries of [D3](D03_scope-keywords-function.md).
  - **Depth.** The first 30 results per query in each store. A Google Play search page returned 20–30 results in a probe on 23 October (`sleep` 28, `Periode` 30, `running` 20); deeper results need the paging endpoint under `/_`, which `robots.txt` disallows. Equal depth follows O'Loughlin et al. (2019), who capped the App Store at the number of results Google Play shows "so that neither store is favoured" (digest, stage A). The App Store is also searched to 200 results; this deeper list is used only as a saturation check, reporting how many selected apps would change.
  - **Selection unit: one app per developer.** At each turn of the alternation, the next eligible listing is skipped if its developer (provider) is already in the sample, including the vendors of the preinstalled group. Developers are matched by developer ID within a store and by normalised developer name across stores. Reason: the 22 October pilot gave one developer 10 of the Play top 47 under one policy URL ([evaluation](../App-Selection-Evaluation_2026-10-23.md) §1); RQ1–RQ3 concern what providers communicate, so further apps of a provider add little new communication and reduce the number of independent providers. This replaces the narrower "skip an app already selected through the other store", which it includes.
  - **Additional sensitivity checks**, reported with the result: no developer rule (app-level selection); alternation starting with the App Store; depth 20; App Store depth 200; leave one keyword out; English-only and German-only keywords. For each, the number of selected apps that differ from the main selection.
  - **Google Play product pages limited to 100 (author's choice 2026-10-24, before the Play collection).** Rating counts are only on the product page. The first Play run (23 October, `googleplay-scraper/v003`) received each page in about 5 s and was stopped after 2 h 50 min with no output; the 49 queries reach up to about 1,500 distinct apps. All distinct Play hits within depth 30 are therefore ranked by the install band that the search page shows for every hit (verified 24 October: 30 of 30 hits for `Periode`, 20 of 20 for `running`), ties by best search position, then package ID; only the first 100 get their product page, and these are ranked by rating count as above. Reason: the band is the store's own public use figure and correlated with the rating count at Spearman 0.949 in the 22 October pilot. Check, reported with the result: the install band at the cut-off, how many apps share it without a page, and whether every selected Play app lies above it. **Collector fix found in the same probe:** the parser misses the app ID of the highlighted top result of a search (v003 counted these as cards without an ID; for `running` it was adidas Running, the first hit). `googleplay-scraper/v004` reads the ID from the card's details URL.
  - **As run (24 October 2026).** Play pages showed 8 to 50 results per query (`happiness` and `Schritte` 50), so the depth of 30 also cut Google Play. Name normalisation missed eight providers whose developer name differs between the stores (Clue, Pregnancy+, Alarmy, Leap Fitness, Map My Run, Daylio, MeetYou, BabyCenter); they were matched by hand because the same app appeared under both names (`aliases` in `scripts/app-ranking/v002/config.json`), and accents are now removed in the comparison. Outcome in the thesis, Results, section "Sample".
  - **German exposure check.** For each app selected through Google Play, whether the same app appears in the German App Store search results and with how many German ratings ([evaluation](../App-Selection-Evaluation_2026-10-23.md) §5).
  - **Proxy check** on the Google Play pool of this collection, as above.
- **Reason:** The thesis studies how privacy is communicated by the apps people are exposed to, so exposure is the only criterion the selection needs, and published app studies select on counts, ranks or download bands and not on average ratings. Rating count is the only exposure signal available for both stores and has a published rationale as a download proxy. The Apple count reflects the German storefront; the Play count does not, and no collector setting changes that. Ranking within each store keeps their different counting bases from being compared directly, and alternating between the lists gives both stores equal weight, as earlier reviews did. The floor, the alternation and the starting store are the author's choices, so the thesis reports how far the selected set moves under the sensitivity variants instead of presenting them as correct.
- **Revision of 25 October 2026 (author's direction; replaces store alternation, the worldwide Play count and the 100-page limit):**
  - **Problem.** The Play rating count and installs are worldwide, so the alternation compared a German App Store ranking with a worldwide Play ranking. Among the 100 Play apps chosen by worldwide install band, the German review count ranged from 5 (Mylo) to 99,884 (komoot): worldwide reach is not German reach.
  - **German measure on Google Play.** The product page's embedded review count follows the storefront country, not the interface language (25 October: MyFitnessPal 32,562 with `gl=de`, 2,887 `gl=at`, 454,109 `gl=us`, unchanged between `hl=de` and `hl=en`; Yazio 41,493 / 3,358 / 2,766). It is the only German-market count Google Play publishes.
  - **Score.** German exposure E = s_iOS · r / R + s_Android · v / V, with r the German App Store rating count, v the German Google Play review count, R and V their sums over each store's candidates (first 30 results of the 49 queries), and s the shares of iOS and Android among German mobile users (StatCounter, read at collection). An app listed in both stores is one candidate whose two parts add up. The term r / R is the app's share of the German health-app attention on its platform; weighting by s counts each platform by its German users, so iOS and Android apps are ranked on one scale.
  - **Selection.** One ranking of all candidates by E; one app per developer; stop at the target with the preinstalled apps counted. No rating floor (it never decided a selected app).
  - **Play pages.** Every Google Play candidate within depth 30 gets its product page (645 apps), reusing the search of 24 October; the 100-page limit is withdrawn.
  - **Scale amended (25 October, after the page collection and before any ranking was computed).** The pool sums R and V are dominated by off-topic hits that the keywords also find (Google Play: Roblox 314,584 and Minion Rush 122,681 German reviews), so they would distort the weighting between the stores. The two counts are instead put on one scale with the apps listed in both stores: assuming that, on average, an app's German users split between the platforms like the OS shares, E = v + c · r with c = median(v / r) over these apps × s_iOS / s_Android, in units of German Google Play reviews. The pool-sum formula is kept as a sensitivity check. OS shares, September 2026 (StatCounter, read 25 October): Android 72.3 %, iOS 27.7 %.
  - **Checks.** Spearman correlation of r and v for apps in both stores (both German measures should agree); sensitivity with equal platform weights, with the worldwide Play rating count, and with the old alternation.
  - **As run (25 October 2026, `scripts/app-ranking/v003/`).** 1,367 candidates, 123 of them in both stores. c = 0.090 (median v / r = 0.234 over the 114 apps with both counts; Spearman r–v 0.80). 47 apps from 47 providers: 32 in both stores, 9 Google Play only, 6 App Store only. Five more developer spellings (Fddb, Headspace, Macadam, BetterSleep, Babelli) and three translated titles (Monitor Your Weight, Instant Heart Rate, WalkFit) were matched by hand (`aliases`, `pairs` in `config.json`). Providers changed out of 47: pool-sum scale 0, equal weights 5, worldwide Play count 11, earlier selection 10, no developer rule 10. Outcome in the thesis, Results, section "Sample".
- **Would change if:**
  - The supervisor means the average rating, prefers downloads, or asks for a different weighting.
  - A verified German install or rating figure becomes available for Google Play (for example a licensed estimate with a country filter).
  - A larger storefront check shows that Play's global count misorders the German market badly enough to change the sample.
  - The pilot shows that the sensitivity variants change most of the top N.
  - The floor, justified on the real candidate pool, turns out to be too low or too high.

## Endpoint inventory (checked 22 October 2026)

Every interface known to give store data on ratings, reviews, downloads or popularity, including interfaces that `robots.txt` disallows and interfaces open only to an app's own developer. "Regional" means the value depends on the storefront or country requested. Each was called once or a few times, at low volume, for Yazio (App Store, id 946099227) and MyFitnessPal (Google Play, `com.myfitnesspal.android`). The probe scripts are not part of the repository. Ignoring `robots.txt` is the author's decision; store terms of service may also restrict automated access, which the ethics section should address.

**Developer only** (needs the app's own account; not usable for other providers' apps)

- App Store Connect Analytics: total, first-time and redownloads, filterable by territory ([Apple acquisition help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition)); bulk export through the App Store Connect analytics reports API. Ratings and reviews per country or region ([Apple ratings help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/monitor-ratings-and-reviews/ratings-and-reviews-overview)).
- Play Console: installs and uninstalls, by country among other dimensions ([Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/139628)); all reviews.

**Third party** (modelled estimates, paid or rate-limited): Sensor Tower, Appfigures, AppMagic, AppTweak, 42matters. Earlier tests: Sensor Tower public pages rate-limited after 15 of 169 apps; Appfigures required an account for German data.

**Conclusion.** No public interface on either store gives German download counts or a total of written reviews, whichever robots rules are followed. The public market-specific exposure signals are the App Store storefront rating count and histogram, the category chart positions on both stores, and the Play country average and histogram shape.
