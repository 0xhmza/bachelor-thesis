## Strongest Counter-Argument

The study may discover a gap between provider wording and a researcher-built catalogue without demonstrating a gap in an independently established communication requirement. The generic tracker combines functions from different products, then translates literature and argued links into possible consequences. Checking every pathway against a source improves traceability, but source correspondence does not establish that its contextual assumptions hold for each German app, or that every conceivable consequence should be named in every surface.

Applicability is then inferred partly from the same provider disclosures whose completeness is judged. A detailed provider can make more pathways visible and therefore acquire more opportunities to fail the coverage test. A terse provider produces more uncertainty. Reporting two denominators is helpful, but cannot identify the undisclosed systems or prove that differences between providers are communication quality differences. At the other end, one harm mention is credited across a category, while a denial is excluded from coverage. The resulting scalar can therefore erase real omissions and create apparent silence from explicit communication.

Nor does this establish downplaying. A policy may describe necessary data practices and scoped protections without enumerating every downstream possibility; a denial may be accurate; cautious language may reflect uncertainty. The chapter correctly declines to test those propositions. Feedback-based selection also does not recover the communications encountered by most German users. The defensible contribution is a dated, framework-conditional map of captured wording and inspected access, with its uncertainties exposed. Any stronger conclusion about evasive providers, actual protection or user harm would require evidence this design does not collect.

## Ignored or competing alternative explanations

1. **Reference-model distance, rather than withholding.** A provider may communicate what is salient to its functions and surface purpose while the model intentionally enumerates wider possibilities. A remaining communication gap establishes the distance to that reference, not concealment. Anchors: lines 628–655, 927–931, 2097–2099. Minimum response: report the reference and contextual assumptions beside the gap; no provider-intent conclusion.
2. **Different disclosure detail, rather than different underlying risk.** More documented data flows generate more evidenced pathways. Anchors: lines 1782–1799 and 1845–1852. Minimum response: show source-dependent applicability and uncertain scenarios rather than rank providers by unqualified omission totals.
3. **Feedback accumulation, rather than present reach.** Ratings/reviews can reflect age and prompts; the conversion may be stable while still failing to track active German users. The current limitations already say this and make it a non-defect if respected. Anchors: lines 1158–1196 and 2067–2074. Minimum response: call the sample feedback-prioritised or retain the defined score’s name, without “most used,” user-reach or encounter claims. No new sampling study is required for the bounded question.
4. **Scoped risk reduction, rather than failed protection.** A measure can reduce exposure without eliminating a pathway. The current text already defeats the blanket attack that residual risk proves mismatch: lines 1684–1687 and 1831–1840 require claim-matched scope and reject that inference. Minimum response: preserve this rule in every worked example; do not label a partial-benefit claim a mismatch solely because the script still returns a pathway.
5. **Inspection opportunity, rather than surface silence.** Restricted accounts, device dependence and a one-platform route can change which interface wording is captured. Current rules separate barriers, tasks and source retrieval and appropriately limit this inference (lines 1389–1407, 1486–1497, 1864–1874). Minimum response: retain those denominators and report findings as captured-path comparisons, not all-interface differences.

## Missing stakeholder perspectives

The study does not directly observe users’ interpretation, users’ actual search success, affected populations’ priorities or providers’ reasons for their wording. Those perspectives are absent by design, not automatically defects in a descriptive text analysis. Do not infer them from modelled harms, analyst walkthrough judgments or stance labels. Anchors: lines 164–166, 1395–1403, 1481–1484, 2097–2099.

## Unexamined premise

Possible harm is treated as the starting point for deciding what communication is “missing.” Possibility alone does not establish a normative requirement to describe each downstream consequence, nor the optimal detail for each surface. The study need not resolve that normative issue to report a descriptive model–text distance. It must resolve it, with additional argument, before calling that distance communication inadequacy, failure, deception or downplaying of actual risk. Current RQ1’s reference-model wording permits the narrower defensible interpretation (lines 99–101); the opening “A harm can only be called missing if the app could cause it” (lines 31–33, 573–578) is necessary-condition reasoning, not evidence of a sufficient disclosure standard.

## Observations and limits of attack

- No technical harm/security verdict is licensed. This is not an unacknowledged flaw: lines 160–166, 1565–1568, 1687, 1840 and 2097–2099 say so. Requiring an audit would change the study, not repair a contradiction within its stated scope.
- No omission-to-intent inference is licensed. Explicit minimisation can be studied as wording; silence cannot establish strategic downplaying. The strongest defense is that current labels and limitations substantially respect this boundary.
- A single shared reference model is not intrinsically circular or invalid. Its falsifiable weak points are the pathway conditions, source transport, coverage mapping and uncertainty treatment, not its being author-constructed. Independent source checking is a real improvement and was credited above.
- A draft’s proposed second coder, sample allocation and devices must be fixed before the relevant stage. Their current unresolved status is not proof of completed-study unreliability. If second-person pathway checking or the human reference plan is dropped later, this assessment must be revisited.
