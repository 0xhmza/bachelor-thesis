Fitness and health tracking apps collect continuous behavioural and
physiological data from which sensitive attributes can be inferred. Providers
communicate privacy across several surfaces --- policies, store listings,
websites, advertisements, and the interface itself --- but that communication is
organised around downstream data practices: who receives data, for which
purpose, for how long. We lack a systematic account of which privacy
\emph{harms} it names. This thesis asks which harms providers acknowledge or
downplay, and how they frame the protection their privacy technologies offer.
Fifty highly rated fitness and fertility tracking apps are sampled from the
German Google Play and App Store storefronts; every privacy-relevant text they
present is collected and frozen, and twelve are additionally inspected through a
cognitive walkthrough whose tasks are privacy tasks. Coding units receive a harm
type from the Citron and Solove typology, a stance (acknowledged, hedged,
minimised, deflected, absent), and a code for how protection is framed --- as
secrecy or as harm prevention. Four probes carry the central question: whether
inference, breach consequences, incident handling, and any concrete consequence
for the user are mentioned at all. Coding is LLM-assisted with versioned
prompts, and validated for recall as well as precision, because the expected
finding is an absence.
