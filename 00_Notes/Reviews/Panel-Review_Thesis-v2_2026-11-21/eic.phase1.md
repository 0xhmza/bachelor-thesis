**D1 (methodology_rigor).** From an editorial standpoint, this dimension asks whether the study is built so that another researcher could repeat it and reach the same sample and the same counts: the design must be explained, data collection and handling must be traceable, any numbers must be reported honestly, and the materials needed to reproduce the work must be pointed to. It is a mandatory gate, but it belongs to the methodology seat; I will not score it, though I will notice if gaps here undermine the contribution I do judge.

**D2 (domain_accuracy).** This dimension asks whether what the manuscript says about its field is correct: prior studies are described as they actually are, domain terms are used with their accepted meaning, and no factual claim contradicts current evidence. It is mandatory and owned by the domain seat; I do not score it, but a contribution claim that rests on a misread of prior work would also weaken the originality I assess.

**D3 (argumentative_coherence).** This dimension asks whether the central line of argument holds together: the claims follow from the evidence offered, the parts of the work do not contradict one another, and no reasoning error breaks the main thesis. It is mandatory and owned by the devil's-advocate seat with the methodology seat also eligible; I do not score it, though my check that the chapters serve the stated research questions touches the same thread from the structural side.

**D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance).** This dimension asks whether readers from neighbouring fields (for example law, health informatics or human-computer interaction for a privacy study) can follow the framing and definitions, and whether any claim that crosses disciplines is backed up. It is high priority and owned by the perspective seat; I do not score it.

**D5 (writing_and_structure).** This dimension, which I own, asks whether the manuscript is organised and written so that its reader can follow it with little effort: chapters in a sensible order and of a size that fits their role, clear and plain exposition, tables and figures that are introduced and readable, and adherence to the conventions expected of the configured document type, here an IT-security bachelor's thesis. It is a normal-priority dimension, so its problems call for revision but do not by themselves decide the outcome.

**D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution).** This dimension, which I own and which is mandatory, asks whether the work makes an original and significant contribution that suits its intended readership and setting. With no author-confirmed venue, the configured setting is examination as an IT-security bachelor's thesis in usable privacy, with top usable-privacy venues used only as a quality bar and not as a target. I will judge whether the contribution is clear even before final results exist, whether the written chapters visibly serve the stated research questions, and whether the scope of the claims matches the scope of the sample, including any restriction of platforms or app stores relative to the market the claims address.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: chapters in a logical order from context to method to findings, each sized in proportion to its role in a bachelor's thesis, written in plain English with terms defined at first use, with every table and figure introduced in the text, captioned and readable, and with consistent thesis conventions for numbering, cross-references and citations.
what_triggers_block: the organisation stops a reader from following the argument, such as findings presented before the procedure that produces them or sections that the structure promises but does not deliver, or the prose is unclear enough that core rules or choices cannot be understood.
what_triggers_warn: chapter balance is off with one chapter much longer or thinner than its role warrants, material is repeated across chapters, jargon or long sentences hinder plain reading, or tables and figures are poorly introduced, captioned or placed.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: a contribution statement that a reader can find early and check before any coding results exist, research questions that each have a chapter or method step visibly serving them, claims whose scope in platform, store, market and time matches the sample actually drawn, and a level of originality and rigour fitting an IT-security bachelor's thesis in usable privacy.
what_triggers_block: the contribution cannot be stated from the text without the reader supplying it, or a research question has no chapter or method step that serves it, or claims about a market go beyond the platforms or stores actually sampled with no stated limitation; this is repairable by rewriting framing, scope or limitations.
what_triggers_warn: the contribution is stated but diffuse or only recoverable late in the text, a research question is served only partly or implicitly, or a scope restriction is acknowledged but its effect on the claims is not argued.
what_triggers_fatal: the work offers no original contribution beyond restating prior studies, or its research questions cannot be answered by the designed study even after rewriting, so that no revision of framing or scope would make it an acceptable IT-security bachelor's thesis.
