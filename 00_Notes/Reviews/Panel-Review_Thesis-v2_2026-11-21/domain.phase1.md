## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): the study design, the handling of data, the reporting of any counts or statistics, and whether a reader could repeat the work must meet the level expected in peer review for this field. From a domain viewpoint, I only note where a design choice rests on a wrong domain premise (for example a misread legal or platform rule); judging the design itself is not my task, and I do not score this dimension.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by me): every claim must match current evidence in health-app privacy research. Prior studies must be described as they actually are, legal statements (GDPR articles, Article 29 Working Party and EDPB guidance) must say what the sources say, platform mechanisms such as store privacy labels must be described correctly, and domain terms (personal data, special category data, consent, privacy harm, harm typology, threat or harm tree) must be used precisely. Statements that no prior work has done something must survive a check against the existing literature. This is the one dimension I score.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's advocate seat with methodology): the central argument must hold together, the evidence must support each claim, and no logical error may undermine the main line. From a domain viewpoint, coherence depends on whether each research question follows from a gap that really exists; I may comment on that link in prose but do not score this dimension.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owned by the perspective seat): readers from neighbouring fields (law, HCI, health informatics, security) must be able to follow the framing, definitions and implications, and any claim that crosses fields must be backed. I do not score this dimension; domain terms that are wrong rather than merely unclear fall under D2.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): organisation, clarity, quality of figures and tables, and fit with venue conventions. I do not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the editor-in-chief seat): the manuscript must suit the configured venue and add something original and significant for its readers. Whether a contribution is new depends partly on the accuracy of the gap claims I check under D2, but the fit and significance judgement is not mine and I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy

dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether related work covers the main strands of health-app privacy research (privacy-policy analysis, femtech and reproductive-health apps, Google Play Data Safety and App Store privacy labels, privacy-harm typologies, threat or harm trees) with a small but representative set of sources; whether each cited study, GDPR article, Article 29 Working Party or EDPB statement is reported as the source says; whether domain terms are used precisely; whether every absence claim (no prior study does X) holds against the known literature; whether each research question rests on a real, documented gap; and whether the chosen harm typology fits the harm-tree approach it is paired with.
what_triggers_block: One or more central domain claims are wrong in a way that changes the study's motivation or interpretation, such as a misstated GDPR provision or Working Party position used to justify the method, a mischaracterised key prior study, or an absence claim that a well-known existing study directly refutes, but the core study can be kept after the claims and framing are corrected.
what_triggers_warn: Domain content is broadly correct but has repairable gaps: a main literature strand is missing or covered only by a secondary source, a legal or prior-work statement is imprecise without changing the argument, a term is used loosely, an absence claim is stated more broadly than the cited evidence supports, or the fit between the harm typology and the harm-tree approach is asserted without explanation.
what_triggers_fatal: The study's stated contribution or research questions rest entirely on a domain premise that is false, such as a claimed research gap that existing published work already fills in the same scope, or a legal premise that the cited law or guidance plainly contradicts, so that no correction of wording can restore the core claim.
