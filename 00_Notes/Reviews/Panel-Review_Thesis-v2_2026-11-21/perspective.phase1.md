## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat). I read this as asking whether the design, data handling and reporting would let another team repeat the work and reach the same result. From a consumer-protection and risk-communication angle, the parts of rigour that matter most to adjacent readers are whether the coding of provider documents is transparent and repeatable, and whether the data-collection steps respect the access conditions of the sources used. I will not score this dimension; where my reading touches it, I will hand the point to the methodology seat rather than grade statistical or design choices, which are outside my expertise.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat). This asks whether claims match current evidence in the field and whether prior work, terminology and results are represented correctly. For readers from EU consumer law and data-protection enforcement, accuracy also covers legal terms: what transparency and information duties actually require, what counts as a data subject's or consumer's harm, and how regulators and courts have read those duties. I will not score this dimension, but I will flag legal or regulatory statements that a specialist in those fields would dispute, for the domain seat to weigh.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the devil's-advocate seat, also open to the methodology seat). This asks whether the central thesis holds together and whether the evidence supports the conclusions without fallacies. From my angle the key coherence risk in work on disclosures is the step from "a statement is absent" to "communication has failed", which only holds if an explicit standard says the statement should be there. I will not score this dimension or stress-test the argument; that is the devil's-advocate seat's role, and I will only note where a normative premise appears to be carried silently.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by my seat). This asks whether the framing, definitions and implications can be understood and used by readers in neighbouring fields, and whether any claim that crosses into another discipline is backed by that discipline's own standards. For me this means: the normative yardstick for judging what providers should say about harms is named and sourced; constructs such as privacy harm and harm communication are defined so a lawyer or risk-communication researcher can apply them; the inference from document text to what users actually receive is argued, not assumed; the ethics and legality of collecting data from platforms is addressed in terms an ethics board or regulator would accept; and statements about users or practice stay within the populations and platforms the study covers. This is the only dimension I will score.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by the journal-fit seat). This asks whether the manuscript is well organised, clearly written, with usable figures and tables and the conventions of its venue. For adjacent-field readers, clarity of definitions and signposting of where the normative and empirical parts separate are the parts of structure that matter most. I will not score this dimension.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the journal-fit seat). This asks whether the work suits its intended venue and makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. From a practical angle, contribution includes whether findings are usable by regulators, consumer organisations, providers or users, but judging fit and significance for the venue belongs to the journal-fit seat, and no venue criteria are bound for this call. I will not score this dimension.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether the yardstick for judging provider harm statements is stated and sourced (data-protection transparency duties, consumer-law information duties, or risk-communication standards) rather than assumed; whether privacy harm and harm communication are defined so that legal and risk-communication readers can apply them; whether absence of a statement is kept distinct from a communication failure unless the yardstick requires the statement; whether the inference from document content to what users are told is justified and its limits stated; whether data-collection ethics (platform terms, robots exclusion rules, load on services) are addressed in terms an ethics or legal reader accepts; whether claims about users and practice are limited to the platforms and populations covered and the consequences of any excluded group are discussed; whether practical implications for regulators, providers or users follow from the evidence.
what_triggers_block: Provider communication is graded or ranked as deficient, or missing harm statements are treated as failures, with no stated and sourced yardstick that makes those statements expected; or the work makes legal, regulatory or user-population claims that its evidence cannot support, so that an adjacent-field reader would reject the central interpretation.
what_triggers_warn: The yardstick or key constructs are present but only implicit, partly sourced or applied unevenly; or the document-to-communication inference, the data-collection ethics or the population scope is acknowledged but under-argued; or practical implications are asserted without the qualifications an adjacent-field reader would need.
