# D13 — How is the literature searched, filtered and screened?

- **Status:** superseded by [D14](D14_literature-review-v2.md) (2026-10-28, at the author's request: the review no longer rests on OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar). Was proposed on 2026-10-26.
- **Affects:** Methodology, Literature Review; Results, Literature Search; [`scripts/literature-search/v001`](../../scripts/literature-search/v001/); `01_Literature/Systematic-Review/` (since 28 October 2026 in `01_Literature/#_Junk_Backup/Systematic-Review/`; removed with that folder on 30 October 2026, in Git history up to commit `f50e097`); frozen collection `05_Data/raw/literature-search_2026-10-26/`
- **Options considered:**
  1. Targeted scoping review after Arksey and O'Malley, searched by hand in venues and Google Scholar, with no fixed query or filter (the text up to 26 October 2026).
  2. **The staged procedure of Baumann et al. (2025, App. A, pp. 56–57), adapted to the four review questions and to open databases (chosen).**
- **Evidence:**
  - Baumann et al. (2025), App. A, pp. 56–57 (checked in the PDF, arXiv v2): Stage 0 searches five databases (Scopus, Web of Science, Semantic Scholar, ACL Anthology, Google Scholar) plus manually added works with one Boolean query of four term groups (annotation, LLM, performance, domain) over a fixed period; removes duplicates and works with fewer than 3 citations per year (1,592 → 453); Stage 1 screens titles and abstracts against two inclusion criteria and one exclusion (→ 93); Stage 2 extracts fixed fields per paper; Stage 3 selects datasets. Table 6 gives the exact query per database.
  - Supervisor meeting of 13 October: use the literature review to identify capabilities for the harm tree, and give references for every methodological decision; search venues named were PoPETs, CCS, USENIX Security, IEEE S&P, arXiv, ACM, CSCW and Google Scholar ([organized notes](../Meetings/2026-10-13_Supervisor-Meeting_Organized.md), §3).
  - Access, tested on 26 October 2026: Scopus and Web of Science need institutional API keys; Google Scholar has no API and answers automated queries with a bot check; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar answer Boolean title-and-abstract queries without a key (OpenAlex allows page-number paging to 10,000 results without a key, not cursor paging). Both index the venues named in the meeting, ACL Anthology and arXiv.
  - Trial counts before the citation filter: the draft query of Q1 with "risk" alone returned 4,555 OpenAlex records; restricting the harm group to harm, threat, attack and "privacy risk" gave 1,997.
- **Choice:**
  - *Stage 0, search.* OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, titles and abstracts, searched on 26 October 2026. One query per review question, each joining term groups with AND (exact strings in [`config.json`](../../scripts/literature-search/v001/config.json)): Q1 domain AND privacy AND harm terms; Q2 domain AND communication surfaces; Q3 privacy policy AND language-model terms AND annotation terms; Q4 policy, notice or breach notification AND wording terms (vagueness, hedging, reassurance, framing, rhetoric, risk communication, privacy washing, persuasion). Period 1 January 2014 to 26 October 2026; Q3 from 1 January 2022, as in Baumann et al. Works known from the earlier reading rounds that meet an inclusion criterion but were not retrieved are added by hand ([`manual.json`](../../scripts/literature-search/v001/manual.json)).
  - *Duplicates and citation filter.* Records that share a DOI, an arXiv ID or a normalised title (publication years at most one apart) are merged. Records with fewer than 3 citations per year (highest count across databases ÷ years since publication, at least one) are removed. As in the source, recent works are not exempt, and the filter applies to manual additions too.
  - *Stage 1, screening.* Titles and abstracts are screened. Include: I1 documents privacy harms, risks, threats or inferable capabilities affecting users of health apps, fitness trackers or health wearables; I2 analyses the privacy communication (policies, labels, store listings, notices, terms) of such apps or wearables; I3 uses or evaluates language models to annotate, classify or extract information from privacy policies or similar privacy text; I4 analyses how policies, notices or labels present or word data practices, risks or protections, in any domain. Exclude: E1 proposes or evaluates a technical system (protocol, encryption, architecture, detection model) without documenting harms or analysing communication; E2 outside the domain and topics; E3 health-app or wearable work without privacy harm or communication analysis (quality, usability, adoption, features); E4 no usable scholarly record (commentary, dissertation, duplicate, no content); E5 language-model work that does not annotate privacy text.
  - *Stage 2, extraction.* Per included work: review question, domain, material, sampling, coding procedure and agreement, models and validation (I3), harms and capabilities named, findings and stated limits, each with page.
  - *Stage 3, capabilities* (in place of Baumann's dataset selection): capabilities named in I1 works are collected with their source, the data they need and the adversary, as input to the harm trees.
- **Reason:** The procedure makes the search repeatable and its counts reportable, which the hand search of option 1 did not (its per-query hits were never logged). Baumann et al. combine a fixed Boolean query, a citation filter that keeps screening manageable, and explicit inclusion criteria; the same structure fits the four review questions. Open databases replace the subscription and bot-protected ones because only they can be queried reproducibly from this project; they cover the venues named on 13 October. Q3 starts in 2022 for the same reason as Baumann's period: annotation with large language models begins then. The other questions reach back to 2014 because the policy studies the sampling and coding build on start in 2014–2015.
- **Limits:** One person screens; the screening decisions were prepared by an AI assistant from titles and abstracts and must be checked by the author. The citation filter removes almost all works from 2026 (the 2026 gap-search works fell under it), so the closest recent studies enter the thesis through the related-work chapter, not through this review. Database coverage of abstracts is incomplete (some records were screened on the title).
- **Would change if:** the supervisor prefers a scoping review without a citation filter; institutional access to Scopus or Web of Science becomes available (rerun Stage 0 with them); the author's check of the screening changes more than a few decisions (then re-screen with a second pass).
