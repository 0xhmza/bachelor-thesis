# D8 — How do two coders work?

- **Status:** proposed (2026-11-01). **Who the second coder is remains open and needs the supervisor.**
- **Affects:** RQ1–RQ4 · Methodology, Validation
- **Options considered:**
  1. One coder with team discussion, as Malki et al. 2024, p. 4 (no inter-rater reliability). Does not meet the meeting's request for two coders.
  2. Two coders on everything. Not feasible for one bachelor's thesis at 50 apps.
  3. **The author codes everything; a second coder codes the validation set independently under the frozen codebook, and reviews applicability, minimisation and mismatch decisions (chosen).**
- **Evidence:** meeting 2026-10-13, §6 ("use two coders"); McDonald et al. 2019 on when reliability measures fit; Campbell et al. 2013, pp. 4–5, 12: unitization is a source of disagreement, and negotiated agreement can help when one knowledgeable coder is available; Krippendorff 2019.
- **Choice:** units are fixed by a script before coding, so coders disagree about labels and not about units. Coders work independently; agreement is computed before any discussion; disagreements are then settled and recorded. If no second coder is available, the existing fallback applies: the supervisor reviews a sample, including judged non-mismatches.
- **Would change if:** the supervisor names a second coder or asks for a larger overlap.

## Update 2026-11-04 — second coder on the validation sample of units

- **Status:** proposed (2026-11-04, follows D9 update). Who the second coder is remains open and needs the supervisor.
- **Choice:** the author and the second coder code every unit of the D9 validation sample independently, without model output; agreement as Krippendorff's α before discussion (reliable ≥ .800, tentative ≥ .667, below .667 merged or reported qualitatively); settled labels are the reference labels. The second coder also checks applicability, minimisation and mismatch decisions of the sampled apps, checks the reference trees before the freeze (D12) and rates recorded walkthroughs of ten apps (D10). Intra-coder: the author recodes a random tenth of the sample after two weeks. Fallback without a second coder: the supervisor codes strata V1 and V2.
- **Evidence:** Krippendorff 2019; O'Connor & Joffe 2020; Campbell et al. 2013; McDonald et al. 2019; methodology review 2026-11-04 (M-L2, M-L6).

## Update 2026-11-04 (evening) — fallback and workload

- **Status:** proposed (submission review R6). If no second coder is available, the double coding is reduced to a smaller probability sample across all strata (not V1/V2 only), with narrower claims. The second coder's tasks are separated: validation units; tree check; a random sample of applicability, minimisation and mismatch decisions including negative ones; re-rating of ten recorded walkthroughs. The pilot measures the time for each. Identity and capacity still need the supervisor.

## Decision 2026-11-04 (night) — taken at the author's instruction

- **Status:** proposed — decided by the AI assistant at the author's instruction of 2026-11-04 ("finish the TODOs depending on reasoning, best practice, literature and context"); marked orange in the thesis until the author or supervisor confirms it.
- **Choice:** the second coder is a student of a computing programme with no other part in the thesis, trained on 20 pilot units (development material) before coding blind. Budget about 22 hours: 500 units at about one minute (9 h), tree check of 21 pathways (2 h), about 40 sampled applicability/minimisation/mismatch decisions (2 h), ten recorded sessions at about 45 minutes (7.5 h), training (2 h). With fewer hours the fallback applies (smaller probability sample across all strata, units reduced first). The name is entered here by the author.
- **Evidence:** O'Connor & Joffe 2020, p. 5 (double-coding 10–25 % of data units is typical); D9 sample size; walkthrough protocol v8.
- **Would change if:** the supervisor names another person or asks for a larger overlap.

## Decision 2026-11-15 — no second coder (replaces the second-coder rule)

- **Status:** decided by the author, 2026-11-15: "no second coder, just model-model-human; the human is me". Replaces the 11-04 rules above, including the student second coder and the fallback.
- **Choice:** two language models code every unit (D9); the author is the only human. The author checks every high-stakes label without seeing the model labels, codes the 500-unit validation sample blind to model output (these labels are the validation reference), and decides applicability, minimisation and mismatches. Reliability of the reference: the author recodes a random tenth of the validation sample after at least two weeks (intra-coder α). Krippendorff's α is reported between the two models, between each model and the reference, and between the author's two codings.
- **Consequences for the other second-coder tasks (derived, marked orange in the thesis until the author confirms):** pathway and card check before the freeze — each of the two models checks every card and pathway against its source, the author decides each objection; walkthrough — the author re-rates the recordings of ten random apps after at least two weeks without seeing the first ratings; applicability, minimisation and mismatch decisions — the author re-decides a random sample after at least two weeks.
- **Limit (stated in the thesis):** the reference labels rest on one person's reading; agreement with it shows how well the models reproduce the author, not that a second coder would agree.
