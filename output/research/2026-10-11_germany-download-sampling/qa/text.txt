Why a final German-download ranking is currently unavailable
Apple’s public Search API provides metadata, not arbitrary developers’ download counts. Apple download
analytics require authorised App Store Connect access [1, 2]. Google’s embedded install integer, often named
realInstalls, is not documented here as German downloads or as a period-specific count. Setting gl=de
cannot establish that interpretation. These public store values therefore do not supply the requested common
metric.
Appfigures documents modeled download reports with explicit product, country and date dimensions; its
estimates endpoint requires eligible plan access [3]. During public browser inspection, Samsung Health’s
unified profile showed an estimate labelled Worldwide, September 2026, with a rounded display of 310K.
Selecting Germany opened the message “Create a free account to continue”. That message proves sign-in is
required at that point; it does not prove that a free account would unlock complete historical German data.
The user confirmed having no account or export. No account was created or subscription purchased. The
worldwide value was not substituted for German data.
Consequently, an evidence-based numerical final sample cannot be produced from the accessible data in this
session. This is a source-access and measurement gap, not a computational difficulty. The sum and ranking
procedure below is executable when its required evidence inputs are present.

Eligibility, matching and missing data
Before ordering products, verify each of the following with dated evidence:
1. German-store availability and membership in the declared keyword or supplementary discovery frame.
2. Relevant health or body tracking. Device companions remain eligible if they record or display tracking
data, as stated in the current proposal.
3. Free installation and German or English interface text. A translated store description alone is not evidence
of interface language.
4. A reachable, relevant privacy policy. Record a retrieval failure before deciding whether it is genuinely
unreachable; HTTP 200 alone is insufficient.
5. No DiGA listing, checked against the BfArM directory [8]. A medical-device claim alone does not establish
DiGA status.
6. No duplicate product and at least one listing with 1,000 ratings.
7. Verified counterpart coverage: all relevant German iOS/Play listings of the same product are known, or
counterpart absence is evidenced.
Identity matching uses stable IDs, developer/provider identity, product purpose and policy evidence.
A
common provider or similar name alone is insufficient. An Android and iOS version of one verified product
contributes once to the sample, with two download observations. Different products from one provider remain
separate unless the duplicate-product rule applies. Shared policies are linked explicitly. Allocate overlap to
fertility when cycle tracking is a main function.
Missing counts remain missing. For a genuinely single-store product, sum only its verified existing listing; do
not invent a zero for an unknown counterpart. If a known counterpart has no usable count, the combined total
is unavailable. Do not exclude such products opportunistically to make ranking possible: the completeness
gap prevents claiming the final highest-download set across the eligible frame.
